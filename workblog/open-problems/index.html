    Open problems¶

    Last updated: November 2026.

    In short

    The observation side is in good shape; the open problems are on the model side. The main one is that the ensemble is too narrow, especially at depth. Most of the other problems below follow from it.

    1. The ensemble is too narrow¶

    The problems we tried to solve with the observation uncertainty all point to an under-dispersed ensemble. In every calibration σ occupies almost the entire uncertainty budget, because the spread has little to contribute at depth (Results). The single biggest limitation of the current setup: spread is generated only at the surface, through three forcing variables, and reaches depth only by mixing.

    What we know. Parameter perturbation adds spread in the right place but only at amplitudes that cannot be justified (Parameter perturbation). Perturbing air temperature adds nothing, particularly in winter where we were hoping to see an effect on the mixing structure (Geneva CTD).

    First answer: a learned model error. A process noise q on the temperature profile, learned online together with the observation error R, makes the spread follow the error: spread/RMSE 0.26 → 0.98 on Upper Lugano and 0.22 → 0.76 on Ägeri, with lower RMSE on both (Learned R and q, Ägeri check).

    Suggested next steps. Port it to master and test it on the other lakes; 7-day reforecasts to see whether the band holds at longer leads; the deep water in summer, where q drops to 0 and the band is still too narrow. Parameter spread at lower assimilation frequency remains an option.

    2. The forcing ensemble is not centred¶

    The perturbation noise on the wind components U and V has zero mean, but the wind speed does not: every member is windier than the control. In the later CTD runs the ensemble-mean wind speed was 31–63 % above the control's (Centred forcing perturbations), and the operational setup (simplified_final) uses the same uncentred perturbation. The ensemble mean therefore carries a systematic extra mixing that the daily analysis has to correct every day, and that accumulates when the observations are far apart.

    Suggested next step. Rerun simplified_final with center_perturbations and compare RMSE, spread and NIS with the current results; repeat the Lugano deep-mixing run with centred forcing.

    3. The summer thermocline¶

    Most places where the analysis is worse than the free run sit at the thermocline in summer (Results). The error there is structural: the model misplaces the thermocline, and a forcing-generated ensemble rarely contains the right profile. Every σ variant we tried either left it unchanged or made it worse (Experiments).

    The learned model error helps here first: on Ägeri the 12–14 m error drops from 0.36–0.38 to 0.26–0.31 °C (Ägeri check). The price is larger corrections at the thermocline (up to 1.4 °C on Ägeri and 2.0 °C on Upper Lugano).

    Suggested next steps. Check whether the large corrections at the thermocline are physical or shocks (the day after each), and whether a step cap is needed; parameter spread at lower assimilation frequency.

    4. The filter is over-cautious¶

    With the seasonal σ the filter is systematically too cautious (NIS ≈ 0.6): it says it is less sure than it really is, and deep observations are trusted far too little. Calibrating σ fixes the consistency but not the accuracy (Experiments).

    Suggested next step. Revisit σ once the ensemble carries more spread; σ should then not have to fill the whole budget!

    This is now possible: with the learned R and q the model error is carried by the ensemble, and R takes only the misses that do not repeat from day to day. Without q (R split only) R stays physical and there is no runaway as with adaptive σ, but the filter swings to over-confident (NIS 1.76 on Ägeri). With q it is close to consistent (NIS 1.29 on Upper Lugano, 0.67 on Ägeri). Open: why Ägeri ends on the cautious side.

    5. Lakes with surface data only¶

    Several lakes have only a surface station. Surface-only assimilation corrects the surface but damages the layers just below, between about 10 and 35 m against CTD (Surface-only). Nothing we tried on the observation side fixed it.

    Suggested next step. Localization limited to the mixed layer, so that surface information is not pushed below it; or, where possible, at least a few sensors over the upper 30–40 m (assimilating deeper sensor 15 m together or CTD).

    6. Deep mixing¶

    Assimilating long-term CTD casts corrects the deep temperatures but makes deep mixing more frequent, 15 winters instead of 10 on Lake Lugano (Deep mixing).

    Part of this may come from the uncentred forcing ensemble (problem 2).

    7. Ensemble size and evaluation period¶

    Twenty members is what the operational chain can afford, not a statistically defended choice. And every result is scored on 2025, the only year with profile observations on all seven lakes; the fitting-year check covers only three lakes (Fitting year).

    Suggested next step. Score each new year as it becomes available, with the σ fitted on the previous ones. The learned R and q need no fitted σ; only the depth correlation of q comes from one year, and pooling more years would make it a lake property.

    8. Physically defensible parameters¶

    Estimating parameters with the filter does not converge: the parameters follow the seasons and absorb the model's seasonal bias (Joint estimation).

    Suggested next step. Slower updates; and compare the estimated distribution with a traditional calibration and learn model weaknesses.

    9. 3D: horizontal spurious correlations¶

    In 3D one buoy can correct a whole region, but with 7 members spurious horizontal correlations are a real concern, and the first runs point to the need for vertical localization (3D).

    Suggested next step. Vertical and horizontal localization; assimilating the satellite surface temperature using scaling to adapt the vertical resolution.
